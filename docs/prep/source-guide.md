## Problem statements

Hackathon problem statements 

Track 1: Gemini 4 Argon in entertainment

Korea—and Seoul in particular—is a global powerhouse in entertainment. How can you use Gemini 4 Argon, Google AI Studio, Antigravity and our generative media models to create new experiences across music, TV, film, webtoons and gaming?

What Gemini 4 Argon adds to this track

3D scenes, physics and procedural worlds: Argon writes Three.js, WebGPU and Blender scripts across a shared 1M-token context window, generating complete multi-file games and interactive simulations in a single long-decode pass

multimodal orchestration: Argon can act as the game master or showrunner while coordinating gemini-3.8-live for real-time voice characters, gemini-nano-banana-2.1 for consistent sprites and storyboard panels, gemini-omni-1.1-flash for video cutscenes, and lyria-3.5 for adaptive soundtracks and vocals

agentic video understanding: Argon scrubs across long video cuts or gameplay recordings to match audio beats, dialogue timing and visual continuity

1-day build ideas

a playable 3D browser game or procedural world built in Three.js or WebGPU where Gemini 4 Argon generates the mechanics and level geometry, gemini-3.8-live runs unscripted voice characters that trigger in-game function calls, and lyria-3.5 shifts the soundtrack dynamically

an interactive film, K-drama or webtoon production tool that takes a script and character reference sheet, uses Argon to plan scene continuity across a 1M-token storyboard, generates consistent 4K frames with gemini-nano-banana-2.1, and renders voiced scenes with gemini-omni-1.1-flash and gemini-3.8-flash-tts

a live music and stage visual co-producer where performers hum, speak or play a riff into gemini-3.8-live, and Argon coordinates lyria-3.5 stems with real-time WebGL visuals synchronized to the audio

Track 2: Gemini 4 Argon in hard tech

Korea's hard tech sector is surging across semiconductors, robotics, biotech, batteries, data centers, energy and aerospace. How can you build with Gemini 4 Argon to speed up development, verification and simulation across hardware and physical systems?

What Gemini 4 Argon adds to this track

long-horizon verification loops: give Argon a measurable engineering target (such as migrating legacy C++ control code to Rust, optimizing a kernel, or verifying RTL and firmware specs) inside an Antigravity sandbox (antigravity-preview-09-2026), and let it build a test harness and iterate until every check passes

1M-token multimodal synthesis: point Argon at dense folders mixing 500-page datasheets, CAD and PCB schematics, oscilloscope logs, laboratory videos and spreadsheets to cross-reference failure modes in a single context window

physics and spatial simulation: combine Argon's "high" thinking mode (thinking_level="high") with Python execution and 3D visualization to model thermal loads, robotic kinematics, battery degradation or molecular pathways

1-day build ideas

an autonomous firmware and datasheet verifier that reads a microcontroller reference manual PDF alongside a C or Rust driver repository, writes an automated simulation test suite in an Antigravity sandbox, and patches register-level bugs until every test passes

a multimodal laboratory or manufacturing root-cause analyst that takes high-resolution inspection video, sensor telemetry CSVs and maintenance manuals, uses Argon's agentic video understanding to locate the exact timestamp and physical anomaly, and outputs a reproducible diagnostic report

an interactive 3D engineering simulator (for robotic arm path planning, drone aerodynamics or data-center cooling layouts) where Argon computes the physics constraints and renders a live WebGPU view you can test on stage

Track 3: Google AI for social good

As AI capabilities grow, using them for public benefit matters more than ever. How can you build with Gemini 4 Argon, real-time voice and translation, and on-device open models to create practical, positive outcomes in accessibility, health, education, civic services or safety?

What Gemini 4 Argon adds to this track

automated accessibility and workflow verification: Argon can crawl a live web app or public service site with a headless browser, inspect the accessibility tree, group barriers by root cause, and either patch the underlying code or guide a user through the task

zero-barrier voice and 70+ language access: combine Argon's reasoning over dense medical, legal or civic policies with gemini-3.5-live-translate-preview and gemini-3.8-live so anyone can use essential services by speaking in their own language

privacy-first local execution: pair cloud reasoning with Gemma 4 (E2B and E4B) on the device when sensitive personal, health or accessibility data must stay on the user's phone or laptop

1-day build ideas

a hands-free voice and screen navigator for blind, low-vision or motor-impaired users that combines gemini-3.8-live, Computer Use and Argon to read complex websites, fill out multi-step forms with explicit voice confirmation before submission, and generate accessibility patches for site owners

a multilingual civic, legal or healthcare benefits guide that loads hundreds of pages of local regulations and forms into Argon's 1M-token window, interviews a resident by voice in any of 70+ languages via gemini-3.5-live-translate-preview, and prepares a cited application packet with a human review checkpoint

a local emergency preparedness and grid or climate response planner that combines municipal hazard maps, live weather feeds, Google Maps grounding and infrastructure PDFs into clear local response plans and verifiable field checklists

Track 4: Open innovation — autonomous agents and developer tools

1-day build ideas

a self-healing security and dependency migration agent that scans a repository for vulnerabilities or breaking API upgrades, writes a reproduction test in an Antigravity sandbox, patches the call sites with Argon, and outputs an interactive visual diff walkthrough

a legacy codebase modernizer that takes an older desktop, Java or monolithic web app along with a screen recording of how it works, extracts a DESIGN.md and API specification, and rebuilds a tested modern web application in a single autonomous run

a multi-agent research-to-prototype tool where Argon coordinates deep-research-preview-04-2026 to gather technical papers or market data, delegates parallel implementation modules to gemini-3.8-flash subagents, and compiles a live interactive dashboard or simulation

Build with Gemini 4 Argon: 1-day hackathon builder guide

You have one day to build a working application or agent with Gemini 4 Argon (gemini-4-argon), our new frontier model for software engineering, complex knowledge work, and multimodal creation. Gemini 4 Argon unifies input, thinking and output in a single 1M-token context window. It works longer on its own, retains context across large codebases and dense document sets.

What makes a strong 1-day project

Judges score working demos, not slide decks. Strong projects pass 4 tests:

it solves a specific problem for a real person you can name, rather than a generic audience

it does work that needed Gemini 4 Argon: long-horizon coding, autonomous verification loops, multi-file synthesis, 3D and WebGPU generation, or multi-agent orchestration

it keeps people in control when taking actions that affect external systems, money or private data

it runs live during judging with real inputs in under 3 minutes

What to know when calling Gemini 4 Argon

The Interactions API is the recommended endpoint for new projects. One API covers both models and managed agents, supports server-side state (previous_interaction_id) and handles background execution.

1. Control reasoning depth with thinking_level

Gemini 4 Argon uses thinking_level ("low" | "medium" | "high") to control how much reasoning effort the model spends before responding:

"medium" (default): balances speed, cost and first-pass accuracy for interactive coding and agentic tool use

"high": maximizes reasoning depth for multi-file debugging, security vulnerability patching, formal maths and upfront system design

"low": minimizes latency and cost for fast classification, routing and targeted extraction

from google import genai

client = genai.Client()

interaction = client.interactions.create(

    model="gemini-4-argon",

    input="Build a single-file HTML drum machine and synth using the Web Audio API with a 16-step sequencer, tempo slider, and a live waveform visualizer.",

    generation_config={

        "thinking_level": "medium"  # "low" | "medium" (default) | "high"

    },

)

print(interaction.output_text)

2. Shared 1M-token context window and long decode (background=True)

Earlier models capped total output (thinking plus response) at 64K tokens. In Gemini 4 Argon, input, thinking and output share a single 1M-token window:

default output limit is 65,536 (64K) tokens on the Interactions API for both streaming and non-streaming calls with no code changes needed

to generate past 65,536 tokens (up to 1M tokens minus your input), set background=True and set max_output_tokens above 65536 on the Interactions API

Set max_output_tokens high enough that deep thinking does not cut off your multi-file diff or proof mid-output, and low enough to cap spend on open-ended prompts:

import time

from google import genai

client = genai.Client()

interaction = client.interactions.create(

    model="gemini-4-argon",

    input="Write a complete multi-module physics simulation and test suite.",

    background=True,

    generation_config={

        "max_output_tokens": 262144,

        "thinking_level": "high",

    },

)

while interaction.status in ("in_progress", "queued"):

    time.sleep(5)

    interaction = client.interactions.get(id=interaction.id)

print(interaction.output_text)

3. Clean up legacy parameters

If you are adapting code written for Gemini 2.5 or Gemini 3 models, remove these deprecated settings when calling gemini-4-argon:

replace thinking_budget with thinking_level (thinking_budget is deprecated and returns a warning)

remove temperature, top_p and top_k (custom sampling values are ignored by gemini-4-argon and return a deprecation warning)

remove explicit cached_content calls (implicit context caching runs automatically at a 95% discount on cached input tokens; introductory pricing throug

h 30 November 2026 is $2 per 1M input tokens and $10 per 1M output tokens)

increase HTTP client timeouts to 15 to 20 minutes for complex "high" thinking prompts, or run them asynchronously with background=True

4. Run Gemini 4 Argon inside Managed Agents

You can pass "model": "gemini-4-argon" to the Antigravity managed agent (antigravity-preview-09-2026). Google provisions a hosted sandbox where Argon plans, writes code, verifies its own output, searches the web and calls MCP tools in a single API call:

from google import genai

client = genai.Client()

interaction = client.interactions.create(

    agent="antigravity-preview-09-2026",

    agent_config={

        "type": "antigravity",

        "model": "gemini-4-argon",

    },

    input=(

        "Audit https://web.dev for performance, Core Web Vitals, and SEO. "

        "Query Google's PageSpeed Insights API for both Mobile and Desktop strategies. "

        "Check search indexing with Google Search for site:web.dev. "

        "Format the output as a side-by-side scorecard table with prioritized fixes."

    ),

    environment="remote",

)

print(interaction.output_text)



When your agent needs API tokens (such as GitHub or Stripe), register them with the Credentials API (client.credentials.create) and restrict the sandbox network allowlist (environment={"type": "remote", "network": {"allowlist": [...]}}) so secrets are injected at the proxy and never enter the model context.

5 patterns that save hours in a 1-day hackathon

Align on intent before long runs. Share your goal with Argon and ask it to surface clarifying questions, edge cases and architecture trade-offs before writing code. Once you agree on the plan and confirm it can reach your tools and docs, step back and let Argon execute the multi-file build to completion instead of prompting turn by turn.

Give Argon a verification loop. Argon performs best when it can test its own changes against a concrete pass or fail signal. Point it at your test suite, compiler output or runtime logs, or ask it to write a headless Chrome verification script first, record a baseline score, and iterate in a loop until every check passes with zero regressions.

Use multimodal references and agentic video understanding. Pass 1 or 2 reference images when building frontends or 3D scenes (Blender, Three.js, WebGPU), or ask Argon to extract typography, color palette and layout rules into a DESIGN.md file first. When debugging UI or timing bugs, record a short screen-share talking through the issue alongside your console logs; Argon's agentic video understanding scrubs across timestamps, inspects high-resolution frames and patches the underlying components.

Orchestrate Argon with Flash and media subagents. Use gemini-4-argon (thinking_level="medium" or "high") as the lead architect to decompose the problem, define interfaces and review diffs, while delegating parallel leaf modules and high-frequency checks to gemini-3.8-flash subagents. Let Argon call gemini-nano-banana-2.1 for UI textures and sprites, gemini-3.8-live for real-time voice interaction, and gemini-3.8-flash-tts or lyria-3.5 for voice and music.

Capture codebase quirks and visual explainers. When Argon solves a non-obvious setup or build quirk, ask it to write the lesson into AGENTS.md or a reusable skill so your team never hits the same snag twice. Before your final demo, ask Argon to generate an interactive HTML architecture diagram or pipeline walkthrough of what you built.
